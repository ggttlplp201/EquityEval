## Rules for pilot results and graphs

- Maintain core reported losses, cash, debt and dilution facts even when a valuation ratio cannot be used. An unavailable P/E is not a bad score.
- Use existing deterministic rules only when their source, denominator and period conditions pass. Distinguish **not meaningful**, **not applicable**, **missing** and **unsupported**. Profile availability must not redefine applicability to hide gaps.
- A profile record should expose identity/version, evidence, effective/known dates, lifecycle and instrument scope, segment overlays, metric/peer/valuation policies and a correction path. This is a design requirement for the later shared-contract review, not a new schema here. A correction produces a new analysis version; it cannot rewrite the old one.
- “Why did this change?” should first show matched periods/figures and deterministic arithmetic. Business causes are separate, cited claims or hypotheses with alternatives and uncertainty. Correlation, revenue growth and stock moves do not establish causality.
- The seven stocks are a **selected-company watchlist**, not a complete sector universe and not one suitable peer cohort. Show business-model composition, exclusions and sample counts. CRCL/COIN/HOOD/MSTR should not become interchangeable because of a tokenization theme; USAR/MP need project-stage and product comparability. Preserve X1 total-versus-median semantics, coverage gates and source access.
- No Bitcoin-miner metrics are required for the initial pilot. Keep semiconductor/hardware, subscription software, asset managers, directly held tokens/securities and energy production/infrastructure/utilities/developers as separately reviewed roadmap profiles, not silently enabled generic analysis.
- Keep company history selectors **3/5/10 years**. Short listing histories, acquisitions, accounting changes or missing mappings may prevent the requested comparable window; show the limitation instead of shortening or manufacturing history.
